10 On-Page SEO Mistakes Indian Businesses Make on Their Website

Most Indian business websites are making at least 3 of these critical on-page SEO mistakes that are silently killing their Google rankings. Are you making them too? Read this before you spend another rupee on paid ads.

Search engine optimisation doesn't have to be complicated. Yet most Indian business websites get the basics completely wrong — and pay for it every single day with low rankings, poor visibility, and missed customers who went to competitors instead.

The good news? On-page SEO mistakes are completely fixable. Here are the 10 most common ones we see when auditing Indian business websites — and exactly how to fix each one.

Mistake 1 — Missing or Duplicate Title Tags

Your title tag is the single most important on-page SEO element. It tells Google — and users in the search results — exactly what your page is about. Yet many Indian business websites leave title tags as the default CMS placeholder, use the same title on every page, or write titles that are too long.

  • Ideal length: 50–60 characters — not too long, not too short
  • Must include: Your primary keyword and your brand name
  • Must be unique: Every single page needs a completely different title tag
Fix it: Open each page of your website in a browser and look at the tab — that's your title tag. Make sure every page has a unique, keyword-relevant title under 60 characters. If they're all the same or say "Home" or "Page 1" — fix them immediately.

Mistake 2 — No Meta Descriptions

Meta descriptions don't directly affect rankings — but they dramatically affect how many people click on your result in the search results. A compelling meta description is the difference between someone clicking your listing or your competitor's right below it. Many Indian websites leave these completely blank, causing Google to auto-generate unhelpful, random snippets from the page content.

Fix it: Write a unique meta description of 140–160 characters for every page. Include your primary keyword naturally and write it like a small advertisement — because in the search results, that's exactly what it is.

Mistake 3 — Wrong Heading Hierarchy (H1, H2, H3)

Your heading tags should form a clear logical hierarchy — like the outline of a well-structured document. Every page must have exactly one H1 (your main keyword and topic), followed by H2s for main sections, and H3s for sub-sections. Common mistakes include having multiple H1s on one page, using headings purely for styling purposes, or skipping heading levels entirely.

⚠ Common Error: Many WordPress themes automatically make the site name or logo an H1 — meaning your page content ends up with either no proper H1 or two H1s competing. Always check your page source code to confirm the heading structure is correct.

Mistake 4 — Images Without Alt Text

Google cannot see images — it reads the alt attribute to understand what an image depicts. Websites with blank alt attributes are completely invisible to Google Image Search and miss an important on-page signal. Every image on your website needs a descriptive, honest alt attribute that explains what the image shows — incorporating relevant keywords where they fit naturally.

For a photo of your team: "YourBrand web design team in Bengaluru" — not just "image1.jpg" or left blank.

Mistake 5 — Slow Page Speed

Google has officially confirmed that page speed is a direct ranking factor. In India, where the majority of users access websites over mobile networks, slow loading dramatically increases bounce rates — hurting your rankings even further. A website that takes more than 3 seconds to load is simultaneously losing visitors and losing rankings.

  • Compress and properly size all images before uploading to your website
  • Use a reliable, fast hosting provider — avoid the cheapest ₹99/month shared plans
  • Enable caching on your website to serve pages faster to repeat visitors
  • Minimise unnecessary plugins and external scripts that slow down every page load

Mistake 6 — Not Mobile-Friendly

Google uses mobile-first indexing — meaning it crawls and ranks the mobile version of your website first. With over 75% of Indian internet traffic coming from smartphones, a non-mobile-friendly website is both a user experience disaster and an SEO disaster. Google's ranking algorithms actively deprioritise websites that deliver a poor mobile experience — regardless of how good the desktop version looks.

Fix it: Test your website using Google's free Mobile-Friendly Test tool. Better yet — open your own website on your smartphone and navigate it exactly as a real customer would. If anything looks broken, tiny, or hard to tap — that's lost business. Learn more about our responsive website design services.

Mistake 7 — Thin or Duplicate Content

Pages with very little content (under 300 words) or pages that duplicate content from elsewhere on your website send very weak signals to Google. Each page needs substantial, original content that genuinely serves the user's search intent. Service pages with only 40–50 words of text are almost never going to rank competitively in India's increasingly competitive search landscape.

Mistake 8 — Poor Internal Linking

Internal links help Google understand the structure and hierarchy of your website, and they pass ranking strength between pages. Many Indian business websites have almost no internal linking — pages exist in complete isolation rather than forming an interconnected content structure. Every page on your website should link to at least 2–5 other relevant pages, using descriptive anchor text that tells Google what the linked page is about.

Mistake 9 — No Schema Markup

Schema markup (structured data) is code added to your website that helps Google understand your content in much greater detail. For Indian businesses, Local Business schema, FAQ schema, and Service schema are particularly valuable — they can trigger rich results in Google search that significantly increase your click-through rates. Most Indian business websites have absolutely zero schema markup implemented.

Mistake 10 — Missing XML Sitemap

An XML sitemap tells Google about all the pages on your website and how frequently they're updated — making it much easier for Google to discover and index all your content. Without a sitemap submitted to Google Search Console, some of your pages may never be properly indexed. Your sitemap should be submitted via Google Search Console and referenced in your robots.txt file.

The Bottom Line: On-page SEO is the foundation of everything. No amount of backlinks or paid ads can fully compensate for a website that gets these basics wrong.
